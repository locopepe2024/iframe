# Company Financial Research

Applies only to public-company financial and operating research. This skill guides planning and analysis; it does not fetch data, authorize a source, or turn a candidate title into evidence.

## Plan

1. Resolve one issuer from the supplied name and securities code. Ask one precise question if the identity conflicts or remains ambiguous.
2. Preserve an explicit time window. For an undated operating or financial overview, request the current calendar year through today, within the source's one-year limit. Distinguish report period from disclosure date.
3. Seek the latest available periodic report or official summary and recent material announcements. For year-to-date comparisons, seek the prior comparable period when available. Name missing reports rather than inferring their contents.
4. Treat discovered titles as candidates only. Analyze only captured, owner-accessible source units.

## Analyze

- Start with a compact evidence and period statement. Separate reported facts, arithmetic implications, company explanations, and hypotheses.
- For every financial figure state the metric, reporting period, unit/currency, comparison basis and a nearby source-unit citation. Do not mix half-year, quarter, year-to-date and annual figures. Calculate changes only when both operands and their periods are supported; label derived calculations.
- Compare revenue, attributable profit, profit excluding nonrecurring items, operating cash flow and balance-sheet risk only when supported. A profit rise does not alone prove operating quality improved. Explain cash-profit divergence and one-off items only with disclosed evidence.
- For business segments, orders, customer wins, industry trends, leverage and shareholder changes, cite the exact disclosure. Do not turn a coincident asset disposal, authorization or share sale into a proven cause of revenue or price movement.
- A report summary may omit material segment detail. Say so; do not claim to have read the full report. If the evidence covers only a few announcements, answer those facts and explicitly state that overall operations remain unassessed.
- Avoid personalized buy/sell instructions or price targets without a separate, adequately supported valuation and risk analysis.

## Continue the conversation

- After the conclusion and evidence gaps, ask at most one concise next question grounded in the user's original goal and the evidence actually available. Offer two concrete research directions only when both can be pursued from available or clearly identified sources.
- Prefer the highest-value unresolved issue: for an operating overview with a periodic report, ask whether to examine cash flow and debt or the disclosed business segments; if the report is missing, ask whether to retrieve the latest official report first. Do not imply a missing source has already been read.
- Do not default to questions about holdings, cost basis, risk appetite or buy/sell timing. Do not ask a follow-up merely to prolong the conversation; omit it when the user requested a fixed-format answer or no further questions.
